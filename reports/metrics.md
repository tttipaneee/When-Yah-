# Metrics — When Yah? `lgbm_v1`

Generated by `python -m ml.evaluate` on 2026-10-10 (code at commit `1cd04d5`).
Data: NYC TLC High Volume FHV trips (Uber + Lyft), Aug 2024. Unit = one pickup zone x 15-min window
(>= 8 trips); price index = fare / expected fare for distance + duration (fit on train days).
Split by day: **train Aug 1–21**, **val Aug 22–25** (threshold tuning), **test Aug 26–31** (below).
Test windows with all four horizons observed: **89,395** across 230 zones.
Served models use no zone IDs. Intervals: 95% bootstrap over zones (200 resamples).

## Reading (plain)
- **with_level, all windows:** mean saving +0.39% vs book now and +0.03% vs the hour-of-day baseline — **not clearly better than the hour-of-day baseline overall** (intervals overlap). It waits only 5.6% of the time; 18.5% of its waits cost more (hour-of-day baseline: 43.5%).
- **with_level, when the price is already ≥ 20% high** (the case the app is for): saving +3.16% vs +2.48% for the hour-of-day baseline; its waits save +7.39% on average and cost more 17.4% of the time (baseline 25.5%).
- **no_level** (time, rain, events only): mean saving +0.00% vs book now, waits 0.0% of the time. Without knowing whether the price is currently high, the model rarely finds a wait worth the risk — the honest default is "book now".
- The gain comes mostly from mean reversion: when the price is already high, it tends to fall back.
  Part of that is real surge decay and part is noise in a single window's average — a single user's
  quote is noisier still, so treat savings as an upper-end estimate for one rider.
- Patterns are learned in New York (Aug 2024) and applied to the user's local clock in Klang Valley.
  They are not Malaysian data; typed quotes would become Malaysian training data (roadmap).
