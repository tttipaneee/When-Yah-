# Metrics — When Yah? `lgbm_v1`

Generated by `python -m ml.evaluate` on 2026-10-10 (code at commit `482d2e2`). The disclosure paragraphs at the end were edited by hand on 10 Oct 2026 (raw data not on this laptop); see each paragraph.
Data: NYC TLC High Volume FHV trips (Uber + Lyft), Aug 2024. Unit = one pickup zone x 15-min window
(>= 8 trips); price index = fare / expected fare for distance + duration (fit on train days).
Split by day: **train Aug 1–21**, **val Aug 22–25** (threshold tuning), **test Aug 26–31** (below).
Test windows with all four horizons observed: **77,629** across 223 zones.
Served models use no zone IDs. Intervals: 95% bootstrap over zone x day blocks (200 resamples).
**Noise control:** each window's trips are split at random into halves A and B. The level the model
sees comes from half A; every outcome (ratio, drop, saving) is measured on half B, so sampling noise in
the current window cannot masquerade as a predictable price drop.

## Reading (plain)
- The old q90 rule (retired) did not beat the hour-of-day baseline; it almost never advised waiting. The served odds rule is assessed at the end of this report.
- (old q90 rule) with_level: mean saving +0.03% (hour-of-day baseline +0.29%); it waits 0.5% of the time, saving +6.26% when it does, but 29.1% of its waits cost more. When the price is ≥ 20% high: +0.26% vs +1.09% for the baseline.
- (old q90 rule) no_level waited 0.0% of the time.
- Forecast error at +30 min is -0.0% vs persistence and P(drop) AUC is 0.59: 15-minute price changes in this data are mostly unpredictable from time, rain and the current level.
- An earlier version of this report (commit 172d4a1) showed larger savings; the leakage audit found
  that most of it was sampling noise (the same noisy window average was both input and outcome).
  Those numbers are withdrawn. The p10–p90 band (≈ 80% coverage) is still an honest range.
- All outcomes are window averages of ≥ 4 trips, not one rider re-quoting the same trip; a single
  rider's band and drop chances may differ (likely a narrower band).
- Patterns are learned in New York (Aug 2024) and applied to the user's local clock in Klang Valley.
  They are not Malaysian data; typed quotes would become Malaysian training data (roadmap).
- **"Wait" rule, no_level** (best of 15–60 min if odds ≥ 1.0× usual and chance ≥ 20%; k picked on val): saving +0.06% (+0.05% … +0.08%) vs book now → **beats book now: the app may say "Wait"**; loses to the hour-of-day rule (+0.29%; paired difference -0.22%, 95% CI -0.26% … -0.19%). When it waits it saves +1.60% on average and 42% of waits cost more. 
- **"Wait" rule, with_level** (best of 15–60 min if odds ≥ 1.0× usual and chance ≥ 20%; k picked on val): saving +0.20% (+0.17% … +0.23%) vs book now → **beats book now: the app may say "Wait"**; loses to the hour-of-day rule (+0.29%; paired difference -0.09%, 95% CI -0.13% … -0.05%). When it waits it saves +2.25% on average and 39% of waits cost more. When the price is ≥ 1.5× usual (255 windows): it waits 94% of the time and saves +4.34% (+2.62% … +5.90%) — about what the hour-of-day rule (+5.13%) or simply always waiting 30 min (+4.06%) get: high prices tend to fall back (mean reversion); the model adds little there.

**Disclosure.** The gate for showing "Wait" was changed on 10 Oct 2026 after we saw test results. The original gate required beating both "book now" and the hour-of-day rule; the rule failed it. The new gate (beat "book now" only) was chosen with knowledge of the test numbers, so treat the "Wait" result as exploratory.

**Other changes made after seeing test results (10 Oct 2026).** (1) The 20% minimum chance of a ≥ 10% drop before saying "Wait" (product rule; re-scored on the same test days — the numbers above include it). (2) Never "Wait" while the expected price at that horizon is not below the price now (the app had shown "Wait" next to a rising forecast line). This guard is served in the app but is **not in the numbers above and untested on held-out days**: we chose not to re-score the same test days again. It can only turn a "Wait" into "Book now"; no saving is claimed for it. Each change re-used the same test days (Aug 26–31), so the "Wait" numbers are exploratory, not a clean held-out result.

**k picked on val:** no_level = 1.0, with_level = 1.0. This is the lowest value tried (1.0), so the odds-multiple test never blocks a "Wait"; the 20% minimum does the work.

**Level in training vs in the app.** In training, level = trip fare ÷ expected fare for its distance and duration (fit on train days). In the app, level = the user's quote ÷ the usual price they type. Similar in spirit, not the same quantity.

**Audit fixes after this report was generated (10 Oct 2026, not re-scored).** (1) Calibration cap: an isotonic top step that jumps more than 0.2 above the step below is capped at that step (with_level +15 min jumped 0.346 → 1.0 on a handful of val windows, and the app showed "100% chance"); chosen after seeing the app, not the test numbers; the tables above are without the cap. (2) Isotonic calibration and k are both fit on the same val days (Aug 22–25), so k is chosen on in-sample calibrated probabilities. (3) Two doubleheader second games (mlb-745713, mlb-745708) carry the first game's scheduled start (16:10 / 13:10; real first pitch 19:37 / 16:42), so their model event features (scheduled start + median duration) are misaligned; source rows: `data/events/nyc_events_2024-08.csv`. Event features are not served for Malaysia.
